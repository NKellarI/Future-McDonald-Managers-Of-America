Interview 3

Weekly Mailer & Fixed-Income Shopper

Understanding how a senior shopper transitions from paper circulars to mobile price comparisons.

Interview Information

Date: October 12, 2026

Interviewer: Jayden West

Interviewee: Jayden's Grandmother

Duration: 20 minutes

Interview Type: In-Person

Product: Smart Shopper

Interview Overview

The purpose of this interview was to understand how an older shopper on a fixed budget plans weekly grocery trips using mailed store ads and what would make a mobile price comparison app accessible and helpful for her.

The interviewer described the Smart Shopper concept, explaining how she could type in her weekly grocery list on her phone and immediately see how much those items cost at Kroger, Walmart, and Target without flipping through paper ads.

Questions and Responses

Every Wednesday, I check the mailbox for the weekly Kroger ad. I sit at the kitchen table, circle the items on sale, like meat, butter, coffee, or fruit and write out my grocery list on paper so I know roughly what I'm going to spend before I go to the store.

Sometimes the paper ad isn't delivered on time, and other stores like Walmart don't send paper ads anymore. Also, grocery store apps have tiny text, pop-ups everywhere, and confusing digital coupons that are hard to figure out on my phone.

I would love it as long as it is simple and easy to read. If I can just type in "milk, eggs, flour, and coffee" and see right away whether Kroger or Walmart is cheaper for my list, that would save me a lot of time and money.

Both! I need to see the total cost so I stay within my weekly budget, but I also like checking expensive items like coffee or meat individually to make sure I'm not overpaying for anything.

Yes, if the buttons and checkboxes are big enough to tap while I'm pushing a grocery cart. I don't want to accidentally tap the wrong thing on my screen and lose my list.

Key Findings

  • The user relies on weekly grocery store ads to budget before leaving home, but paper ads from many stores are disappearing.
  • Existing grocery store apps feel too cluttered and difficult to navigate on a phone screen.
  • Both overall store basket totals and individual item prices on expensive staples are important to her.
  • Large text, clear buttons, and an uncluttered in-store checklist are essential for usability.

Product Requirements Identified

  1. Clean, easy-to-read interface with large text and buttons.
  2. Side by side comparison of different store prices in one place.
  3. Estimated store total calculation for weekly budgeting.
  4. Individual item price breakdown for high-cost pantry staples.
  5. Simple in-store checklist view with large checkboxes.

Conclusion

This interview shows that senior and fixed-income shoppers who traditionally budget with weekly Kroger mail ads want a straightforward way to compare prices on their phones. By keeping the interface uncluttered and clearly displaying both individual item prices and total cart estimates, Smart Shopper can replace paper ads without overwhelming the user.
