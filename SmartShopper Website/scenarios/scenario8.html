Smart Shopper All Scenarios

8. Reviewing Potential Savings

Persona: Jordan Lee - Family Grocery Planner

Overall Objective

Review potential savings across stores for a 30-item family barbecue list to decide whether one-stop or split-store shopping is worthwhile.

Scenario Narrative

Jordan is preparing for a weekend family barbecue and creates a 30-item grocery list in Smart Shopper that includes ribs, burger patties, buns, produce, and bulk sodas. When Jordan compares store prices, Walmart has the lowest single-store total at $142.00, while Kroger is close behind at $146.50, and Target is $158.20—showing a $16.20 single-store savings by choosing Walmart over Target. Curious why Walmart and Kroger are so close, Jordan checks the potential savings breakdown and sees that Kroger is much cheaper on meat and produce this week, whereas Walmart is cheaper on dry goods, chips, and sodas. Smart Shopper shows that splitting the trip between the two stores (located across the street from each other) drops the combined total to $119.25, saving $22.75. Previously, calculating potential savings across large lists required tedious mental math.

Expected Outcome

Jordan clearly sees both the single-store savings ($16.20) and the multi-store split savings ($22.75) to make an informed decision for the family budget.

Design Considerations

  • Display the exact dollar difference between the cheapest and most expensive store totals.
  • Highlight category and item-level savings that explain why one store is cheaper.
  • Provide an optional split-trip savings view for shoppers willing to visit two nearby stores.
